The Case for Upzoning

Why transit corridors are Chicago's greatest untapped asset

Upzoning means allowing property owners to build taller, denser buildings on land that's currently restricted to low-rise or single-family use. When applied strategically — near CTA L stations where residents can walk to reliable public transit — it creates housing capacity without the traffic, parking costs, and infrastructure strain that come with sprawl.

Transit corridors are ideal for density because the infrastructure already exists. A resident living five minutes from an L stop is less likely to need a car, less likely to add pressure to local roads, and more likely to support the neighborhood businesses that thrive on foot traffic. Every major city that has successfully added housing — from Tokyo to Minneapolis — started by relaxing density limits along transit lines.

The data supports Chicago following the same path. Our analysis of over 25 years of Cook County assessment records shows that properties near L stations consistently appreciate faster than comparable properties farther away. Zones that already allow moderate density — low-rise apartments, mixed-use buildings — outperform single-family areas on long-term value growth. Upzoning doesn't just add housing supply; it amplifies the economic value that transit proximity already creates.

Key Findings

What 34,701 parcels tell us

🚉

5,896 parcels within a 5-minute walk of an L station

That's 17% of analyzed parcels sitting in Tier 1 transit access zones — under 400 meters from a station entrance. These properties score highest for transit-oriented development potential and represent the strongest candidates for denser, mixed-use zoning.

🏘️

82% low-density residential, 28.9% are upzoning candidates

The vast majority of parcels in the study area are zoned for single-family or low-rise residential use. Nearly one in three meet the criteria for upzoning — transit proximity, current under-utilization, and compatible neighborhood characteristics.

📈

19–23% projected appreciation above baseline over 10 years

Under moderate upzoning scenarios, residential parcels near transit are projected to appreciate significantly beyond baseline growth rates. This is value created by better land use — not speculation, and not displacement.

Analysis Deep Dives

How we built the evidence

Historical Appreciation

48.8M records · 1999–present

Over 25 years of Cook County assessment data, analyzed by zone type, to establish baseline appreciation rates and identify which zoning categories generate the strongest long-term value growth.

Upzoning Uplift Model

3 scenarios · per-parcel projections

Three upzoning scenarios — residential, commercial, and transit-oriented — applied to every candidate parcel, with 10-year value projections based on historical appreciation differentials between zone types.

Transit Proximity Scoring

144 CTA L stations · 4 access tiers

Every parcel scored against all 144 CTA L station locations, classified into four distance tiers. Tier 1 (under 400m) through Tier 4 (over 1,200m), each with distinct development potential profiles.

Soldier Field Redevelopment

Scenario analysis · view map

A thought experiment: what if the Soldier Field site were redeveloped at the density of its Near South Side neighbors? The analysis projects value yield per acre using actual neighborhood assessment data.

Methodology & Sources

How this analysis was produced

Data sources: Cook County Assessor's Office (48.8 million historical assessment records from 1999 to present), City of Chicago zoning GIS boundary files, and CTA L station location data. All datasets are publicly available through the Cook County and City of Chicago open data portals.

Analysis tools: Geospatial processing was performed using Python with GeoPandas and Shapely for spatial joins and buffer calculations. Statistical analysis used Pandas and NumPy. Interactive maps are rendered with Deck.gl and PyDeck.

Limitations: The current uplift model uses a flat $250,000 baseline per parcel rather than actual market values. Incorporating per-parcel assessed or market values from the Cook County Assessor would significantly improve projection precision. Transit access scores reflect straight-line distance to station entrances; actual walking distance may vary.
