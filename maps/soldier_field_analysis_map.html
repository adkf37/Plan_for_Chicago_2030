Chicago's next premier lakefront neighborhood

Walk from the South Loop to the sand on 14th, 16th or 18th. The proposal aims for two miles of accessible public beach and $12 billion in property value at full buildout.

The proposal

Reconnect the grid

Build a deck above the active rail tracks in their existing alignment. Carry sidewalks, cycling routes and short blocks across the deck. Remove the district segment of Lake Shore Drive and extend 14th, 16th and 18th streets east to public beach entrances.

Build on the former harbor

Remove Soldier Field and its southern parking facilities. Fill Burnham Harbor and lay out streets across the new land. Roughly 40-story residential towers would rise above lower buildings with shops, frequent doors and courtyards.

Keep the shore public

Clear the structures on Northerly Island and the Adler parking lot. The working plan retains Adler Planetarium. Reserve a continuous beach and park along the open lake, using 150 feet as the working width and testing 100 and 200 feet where conditions differ. The schematic shoreline line currently measures about 0.9 mile. The two-mile beach goal requires a longer mapped alignment.

What comparable blocks are worth

Wabash to Michigan, Roosevelt to 14th covers 13.25 gross acres, measured between approximate street centers. The 2023 Board of Review total is $52.78 million. Converting each assessment at its property-class rate gives a market equivalent of $471.92 million.

Of 986 tax accounts, 5 are classified exempt. They remain in the acreage and contribute zero assessed dollars. The count measures tax accounts, which can include individual condo units and parking spaces.

$35.62 million per gross neighborhood acre

Apply this benchmark to the acres ultimately allocated to comparable blocks and their local streets. The approximately 344-acre study perimeter also includes dedicated public shore, civic space and major infrastructure. The district total depends on that allocation. At the 2023 benchmark, $12 billion would require about 337 comparable acres within the 344-acre study perimeter. The final land budget and tower program will determine how much property value the district can support.

A check using Strong Towns Chicago's 2024 mapped values gives $38.12 million per acre over these same street boundaries. Our calculation using 2024 County records gives $38.16 million. Explore the parcel-level map.

Deck construction, harbor fill and shoreline works require their own cost estimates. McCormick Place redevelopment and citywide Drive removal are later proposals.

Neighborhood illustrations

Forty stories. Walkable blocks. Public beaches.

Imagine turning off a busy shopping street and seeing Lake Michigan at the end of the block. Towers rise above shops and courtyards, while the public promenade follows the beach.

Height alone does not determine value. A 40-story scenario needs tower count, floorplates, podium area, street and public-space allocation, and market evidence. Beach feasibility, access and cost are a separate part of the proposal.
