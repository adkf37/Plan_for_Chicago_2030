## What the One Central study contributes

The supplied May 2025 document is KPMG's feasibility review for Illinois DCEO. Page 10 maps Landmark's approximately 34-acre site west of Lake Shore Drive, between Roosevelt Road and McCormick Place. It describes several land and air-rights interests, including Metra and MPEA. The rail site overlaps our western edge, so adding 34 acres to our perimeter would count land twice. [Study, pp. 5?10](one-central-feasibility-study-2025.pdf#page=10)

The 2019 podium design provides the closest precedent for decking. The revised 2024 linear design would move the tracks west, reduce the overbuild, cut the program from 22.9 to 18 million square feet and reduce the requested state contribution from $6.5 billion to $2.75 billion. The latter sum is a funding request for a broader civic program. Neither figure prices a deck over tracks kept in place. The full deck is the design choice here because it lets 14th, 16th and 18th cross the rail corridor and restores continuity between those crossings. Compare the cost and walking benefits of its phases while holding that choice fixed. [Study, pp. 8?15](one-central-feasibility-study-2025.pdf#page=9)

Page 14 identifies the practical interfaces with electrified rail, yard and shop access, busway operations and utilities. Develop the deck section and construction sequence with the operators before assigning tower loads or timing the first street opening. Page 22 gives Landmark's 20-year buildout assumption. The consultant flags uncommitted transit services, changed demand and long-term public operating obligations, and tests a case in which only the civic phase opens. For this proposal, calculate the value and walking access at each street opening, alongside deck and shoreline costs incurred by that date. ## District design

From the west, a pedestrian on 14th, 16th or 18th Street would cross a deck above the operating railway, pass through the former Drive corridor and reach new neighborhood streets on the stadium and harbor sites. The deck supports the crossings and the blocks between them. Each street would end at a public entrance to the lakefront park. The proposal keeps train service on its existing alignment throughout the completed district.

The filled basin would carry a mix of residential blocks, shops and local streets. Roughly 40-story towers would rise above lower street walls with frequent doors, shade and crossings at sidewalk level. At the eastern edge, the built blocks give way to planted parkland, walking and cycling paths, dunes and a sandy beach. The aerial illustrates this arrangement. Its building count and street locations are illustrative. [Proposed aerial view](../site/assets/soldier-field/filled-harbor-aerial.png)


| Intervention | Treatment in the proposal |
|---|---|
| Rail corridor and Lake Shore Drive | Deck the operating tracks in place. Remove the Drive within the district. Map replacement bus, service, emergency and through routes before estimating transportation effects. Citywide Drive removal is a separate proposal. |
| Soldier Field and adjacent parking | Remove the stadium and reuse its site and southern parking areas for streets, blocks and civic space. Carry forward the 40-story, walkable-block concept. |
| Burnham Harbor | Fill the harbor to create land for development. Survey the basin and inventory berths, navigation, water exchange and habitat. Budget fill, settlement time, foundations and shoreline works separately from tower construction. |

The sketch preserves the planetarium building, interpreting the explicit parking-lot instruction as a museum exception. | East?west connections | Extend 14th, 16th and 18th streets across the deck and former harbor to public beach access points. The map shows schematic corridors. Resolve grades, block crossings and safe walking and cycling approaches in the next street layout. |

The Park District identifies an existing public beach south of Adler and native prairie, wetland and aquatic habitat on the island. Identify what each clearance or fill phase displaces and where those public functions would go. The feasibility review for construction in Lake Michigan also needs an ownership and public-trust review, aquatic impacts and a federal permitting assessment. ## How wide should the public shore be?

Carry 100 feet as the narrow option and draw 150 feet as the working section, widening to 200 feet at street ends and around the existing beach where the shore allows. At 150 feet, a concept section might allocate 75 feet to sand, 45 to planted dunes and 30 to accessible walking and cycling paths. Actual beach profile, fluctuating water levels, habitat and erosion will determine workable widths. The Park District documents past high water covering the southern part of 12th Street Beach. [Park District shoreline assessment, segment 33](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/media/50641/download?inline=)

| Continuous width | Public acres per shoreline mile |
|---|---:|
| 100 ft | 12.12 |

Each additional 50 feet takes about 6.06 acres per mile. Measure the final shore alignment and park polygon before subtracting public acreage. The Proposed overhead draws the sand, dunes and promenade along the lake-facing edge, with wider public entrances at the ends of 14th, 16th and 18th.

## What the proposed scale is worth

The Wabash-to-Michigan blocks run from Roosevelt Road on the north to 14th Street on the south. Joining the centers of the four mapped intersections gives 13.25 gross acres. All 986 Cook County parcel centroids on or inside that outline matched a 2023 Board of Review assessment. Five accounts are classified EX and carry zero assessed value. They remain in the count and their land stays in the gross denominator. The number of exempt accounts alone does not measure the acreage they occupy.

The Board assessed value totals $52.78 million. Converting each PIN using its assessment class gives $471.92 million of assessment-implied taxable property, or **$35.62 million per gross neighborhood acre**. At the same intensity, ten acres of completed neighborhood blocks and their local streets would represent about $356.2 million of land and buildings on a 2023 basis. The district's comparable neighborhood acreage depends on its final land budget. [Street boundary](../data/reference/soldier_field/wabash_michigan.geojson) / [Benchmark calculation](../data/reference/soldier_field/comparator_audit.json)

Allocate blocks and their ordinary local streets as gross neighborhood acreage. Account separately for dedicated shoreline park, retained civic uses, major infrastructure and any unfilled water. Allocate rail deck and former harbor once, based on their finished uses. This avoids both valuing public beach as towers and deducting ordinary streets twice. A parcel-level building program can then test the density implied by 40-story buildings.
